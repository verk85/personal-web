All my life I've been a gamer. Ever since I played my first video game, I haven't stopped, and I still struggle to put the controller down!

I remember the first game I played: TMNT for the SNES. It was a co-op beat 'em up game I played with my cousins where we worked together to defeat the bad guys and reach the goal. We'd take turns when someone died, all while secretly COMPETING to see who could survive the longest.

I think that pretty much defined my approach to how I see video games and eventually life: competing to see who is best and improving to be the best. Now I'm mostly competing with myself, but improving is still my main objective.

Even though I'm not playing as I used to, I still consider myself a gamer. I enjoy video games and I still play some kind of game daily to be honest, but it doesn't compare to a few years back. There are some games that get me hooked and I can't stop playing them until I accomplish some objective. But hey, that got me to where I am today.

I never kept track of how many hours I spent on games like most platforms do now, but I'm pretty sure that I've spent more time with a controller or a mouse and keyboard in my hands than without them. I've played all kinds of games, on all kinds of consoles and devices. I've grown to enjoy solving puzzles, grinding for exp, building connections and negotiation skills, optimizing resources, improving strategies, memorizing maps, combat and economy systems, etc.

Video games made me conquer those skills through hours and hours of challenges, side quests and leveling up. Curiously those skills translate pretty well into life; the hardest thing to do is to stop using them only on video games. I had a happy childhood, but the way video games can absorb you into so many different worlds is quite amazing. For a little over 20 years, I enjoyed playing as an avatar more than playing life as me.

## The Main Character Role

That changed when I started to treat life as a game and me as the main character.

The first thing I usually do in a game is understand the world, its rules, and its mechanics. I need to know what I can do and how to do it in an optimal way, so I can use it to my advantage and make the best out of my time.

So I took that concept and started to apply it to my life.

## Discovering My Playstyle

I always thought I was bad at school and at doing chores in general. Doing homework, keeping notes, and paying attention for hours, that was a real hassle, though I never struggled with tests. It was not that I lacked attention span, there were a few classes that were interesting to me and I could easily do all the work for them. But when a class didn't feel interesting to me I just couldn't do it. 

I always thought there was something wrong with me, but I kept trying and trying. I was able to get a degree, but I struggled a lot to achieve it. I think I failed more classes than I passed during the regular courses, but thanks to retakes, I was able to make it.

Now that I understand myself better, I realize that I actually work differently, and that there's nothing wrong about that. I enjoy reading, taking notes, I'm very good at self-learning and improving; I just struggle to follow a straight line. I need to let my curiosity guide me. If something catches my attention, I can focus like crazy. When I'm enjoying what I'm doing, I can spend hours, days, or even weeks learning about it, reading, and experimenting. I actually have to force myself to stop, instead of struggling to start.

## Maximizing My Build

I also learned that keeping myself healthy allows me to be more active, improves my concentration and elevates my overall quality of life. So, I started prioritizing exercise, reading, nutrition and sleep. Just like in an RPG, building my character with the best stats and avoiding the bad ones.

I started focusing on my mind and body: what can I do to "max out" my avatar (me)? How can I make a good build? Should I max out intelligence, strength, or dexterity? What skills do I have? Can I learn new ones?

I started to pay attention to how I can improve myself and level up, no matter what. 

Now I read more, do more exercise, eat better, sleep better, and I think I finally understand how my body and mind work. 

## A Brand New Game

It has been very interesting and humbling to realize that I had never truly understood myself and that this was the reason I struggled so much with things that came easily to others.

I'm still learning about myself and will probably never fully figure it out, but I am sure that I will keep improving. This is especially true now that I'm taking advantage of my own system instead of fighting against it.

I feel like I'm just starting a brand new game with endless opportunities, a game where I can take advantage of the rules and mechanics, a game where I can optimize my resources and abilities to make the most out of them, a game where I can take the main character wherever I want.

I'm quite happy with who I am now, but I can't wait to see where I can go.
